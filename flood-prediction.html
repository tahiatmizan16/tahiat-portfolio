Flood prediction with LLMs.

A plan to forecast flood risk for roads in Bangladesh. Numerical models predict river and rainfall conditions, and LLMs help turn flood reports into usable data and clear explanations.

Time-series forecastingLLM document analysisRoad vulnerabilityGIS
01 / GOAL

What I want the project to answer.

Rank road segments by flood exposure over the next 1–3 days, using water-level forecasts, ground elevation and past flood records, and report the uncertainty of each ranking. The output is a plain-language summary and a map, so it's useful to someone who isn't a hydrology specialist.

Future goal: predict washout, when fast water breaks away a road, once data on road material and water speed is available.

02 / DATA

Where the information comes from.

Rainfall

Daily rainfall records

Station records from the Bangladesh Meteorological Department, with satellite rainfall (NASA GPM or ERA5) as a backup for gaps.

River levels

Water level history

River and water-level records from BWDB, used to see how rainfall turns into flood conditions.

Roads

Road network and elevation

Road lines from OpenStreetMap or a local survey, with ground elevation to check which low-lying sections are exposed.

Text

Flood reports and news

Bulletins and reports describing past floods, used by the LLM to pull out dates, districts, rainfall totals and damage.

03 / METHOD

Step by step.

  1. Collect and clean one station's rainfall and water-level history, then check for gaps and errors.
  2. Build a simple baseline: predict tomorrow's water level from today's level, and measure the error.
  3. Train a machine learning model (gradient boosting or an LSTM) on past rainfall and water levels to forecast several days ahead.
  4. Use an LLM to read flood reports and extract a structured table of past flood events for checking the forecasts.
  5. Join the predicted flood extent to the road network in QGIS, and flag road segments above a chosen flood depth.
  6. Write a short plain-language warning for each flagged road, and publish the results.
04 / THE LLM'S ROLE

What the LLM does, and what it doesn't.

Does

Reads and structures text

Turns unstructured flood reports into a table, and answers questions about them with the source sentence shown.

Does

Explains the forecast

Rewrites the model's output as a clear warning for residents and engineers.

Doesn't

Forecast the water level

Numerical models make the prediction. The LLM is not trusted with the numbers, and its extracted data is checked by hand on a sample.

05 / CHECKING THE RESULTS

How I will know it works.

The forecast is compared with the simple baseline on held-out years it never saw during training. The LLM's extracted table is checked against the original reports for a sample of entries, and the error rate is reported alongside the results.
